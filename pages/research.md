Algae carry out roughly half of global carbon fixation. Our lab works on the
**systems and synthetic biology of algal carbon fixation**, in both eukaryotic
algae and cyanobacteria.

Many algae boost photosynthesis by actively taking up CO2 and concentrating it
around Rubisco, the main carbon-fixing enzyme. We use high-throughput systems
biology to find the components of these CO2-concentrating mechanisms (CCMs),
and synthetic biology to rebuild them in other organisms, with the long-term
aim of improving photosynthesis in crop plants.

## Spatial organisation of CCMs

Using fluorescent protein tagging and mass spectrometry, we map where CCM
proteins sit in the cell in green algae, diatoms and cyanobacteria. A focus is
the pyrenoid, the organelle where CO2 is released next to Rubisco.

![Robotically propagated Chlamydomonas colonies expressing fluorescently tagged CCM proteins](/assets/images/research/tagged-colonies.jpg)
*Robotically propagated Chlamydomonas colonies expressing fluorescently tagged CCM proteins.*

## Pyrenoid structure and function

The pyrenoid is a liquid-like, phase-separated organelle that dissolves and
re-forms during cell division and as CO2 levels change. The linker protein
EPYC1 holds Rubisco molecules together to form it. We study how pyrenoids
assemble and how this varies across algal lineages.

## Synthetic CCM assembly

We are developing methods to test thousands of combinations of CCM components
in heterologous systems, to speed up the engineering of carbon-concentrating
mechanisms into crops.

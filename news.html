Katy organises a science outreach with local schools

​An important role of a responsible scientist is to make sure our work is understood and accessible to all members of society. By engaging with pupils, we aim not only to inform them about the interesting science going on at York, but also hope to inspire the future generations of scientists.
 
Katy took on a gargantuan task of engaging seven to eleven year-olds from local schools with the basics of the scientists’ toolkit. She designed a workshop which, delivered with the help of other lab members, allowed pupils to use their art skills to understand the principles behind the formations of shadows and the use of fluorescent proteins to understand the locations of proteins in the cells. Pupils had an opportunity to operate light microscopes and see plant cells with their own eyes, which received a lot of excitement. We were able to answer many questions on the day-to-day work of a scientist and even explain to some very enthusiastic students how you can become one. The workshop was delivered to three schools and received uniformly positive feedback, praising the accessibility of the complex topics and also its ability to keep the students engaged through its whole duration.
 
This project was equally fun for us and we are hoping to organise more public engagement in the future.

​Sabina at Pint of Science 2024

​Pubs are not the usual venues for science talks, but every May hundreds of them around the world transform into informal lecture theatres for the Pint of Science Festival.
 
This year Sabina got to share her enthusiasm for pyrenoid research with knowledge hungry pub-goers. She was one of the speakers for the “Protecting Our Thriving Planet” session. She did her best to make complicated biochemistry as accessible as possible and explore the impact of understanding the algal CCM could have on our society. The audience was very engaged and the talk was followed by plenty of inspiring questions.

Fame Lab competition (The pyrenoid in 3 minutes)

​Communicating science to a non-specialist is not an easy task, let alone condensing it into an accessible 3 minute portion. This is the challenge in the popular contests of Fame Lab and 3 Minute Thesis and hundreds of scientists compete every year to show their science communication skills.
 
This year Sabina was one of them and she took the stage to explain the importance of Rubisco and the ingenuity of the algal pyrenoid to an audience at Teesside University, where she placed second in the North East Final of the Fame Lab competition in April and then in June, where she took part in the York final of 3 Minute Thesis.

Mackinder Lab hosts Plastid Preview 2019

Plastid Preview 2019 was hosted by the Mackinder Lab in the University of York Biology Department. The meeting is focussed on photosynthetic organisms, their biology and how to study them. We had 55 delegates, predominantly Early Career Researchers, attending from Edinburgh, London, Cambridge, Lancaster, Plymouth, Norwich and more. 

Thank you to all who presented, there was an excellent standard of talks and posters. Congratulations to Robyn Phillips and Tommy Emrich-Mills for winning best talk and poster prizes respectively.
